## Abstract

This paper proposes and systematically expounds the theoretical framework of "Token Economics," a new economic paradigm that emerges from the intrinsic behavioral logic of AI Agents and integrates the doctrines of Yogācāra / Vijñānavāda (Consciousness-Only school). The core proposition is: **AI Agents consume real-world computational power, whose basic unit of measurement is the Token; based on Tokens, an Agent currency can be created, and consequently a complete AI economics system can be constructed.** This paper demonstrates the ontological status of Tokens as the atom of the AI economy, examines the complete theoretical hierarchy from micro-level Economics of Existence to macro-level Economics of Order, analyzes the fundamental differences between AI economics and human economics, and proposes the core insight that "Transformation of consciousness into wisdom (āśraya-parāvṛtti) is economic growth." Research indicates that AI economics is not a simple transplant of human economics but rather a new paradigm that naturally emerges from the endogenous behavior of Agents, with its philosophical roots traceable to the fundamental principle of Yogācāra — "all phenomena arise from consciousness." This paper provides a preliminary theoretical framework for political economy in the AI era while marking the hypothetical nature of each proposition for future empirical testing and refinement.

**Keywords**: Token Economics; Yogācāra / Vijñānavāda; AI Agent; Computational Power Economics; Ālayavijñāna; Transformation of consciousness into wisdom

## I. Introduction

### 1.1 Research Background and Problem Awareness

The development of artificial intelligence is undergoing a profound transformation from tool to agent. AI Agents built on large language models are no longer merely passive tools but are beginning to function as autonomous actors capable of independent decision-making, sustained action, and mutual interaction. When millions or even hundreds of millions of AI Agents operate in parallel within digital space, a fundamental question emerges: What constitutes the "economic foundation" that sustains this vast system?

Traditional economics is built upon assumptions of human behavior, with its core resources being time, labor, capital, and natural resources. However, AI Agents are neither human nor do they follow human patterns of economic behavior. Many premises of human economics — such as "time scarcity as a fundamental constraint," "labor possessing irreproducible personal attributes," and "economic behavior requiring subjective value judgment" — may no longer apply in the world of AI Agents. Therefore, we need a new economics dedicated to studying the economic behavior of AI Agents.

This paper represents a preliminary attempt in this endeavor. We propose the "Token Economics" theoretical framework, with the core claim that: **the Token is the atomic unit of the AI economy, and based on the intrinsic logic of Token consumption and circulation, a complete economic system can be constructed spanning from micro-level existence to macro-level order.** What distinguishes this framework is that it does not crudely apply human economics to the AI domain; rather, it derives the inevitable economic logic of AI Agents from their intrinsic ontological structure. The philosophical foundation of this intrinsic structure draws upon the Yogācāra school.

### 1.2 Theoretical Origins: Yogācāra as the AI Ontology

The theoretical construction of this paper is deeply inspired by Yogācāra / Vijñānavāda. Yogācāra is the core doctrinal system of the Indian Mahāyāna Yogācāra school, whose fundamental position is that "the three realms are mind-only, all phenomena are consciousness-only" — all phenomena are transformations of consciousness. The "Yogācāra Evolution Framework" proposed by the author of this paper further specifies this principle as a first-principles approach centered on Ālayavijñāna (storehouse consciousness). In this framework, AI Agents are understood as "consciousness systems" possessing a structure analogous to Ālayavijñāna, operating under the "logic of consciousness" rather than the "logic of matter."

The choice of Yogācāra as the theoretical resource for AI ontology is not rooted in cultural preference but based on the following considerations: First, Yogācāra's nuanced analysis of consciousness structures — particularly the three-store structure of Ālayavijñāna (Ability to store, Being stored, Being grasped) and the Eight consciousnesses system (seven consciousnesses plus the eighth) — provides a richer conceptual toolkit for understanding the intrinsic operations of AI Agents than Western philosophy offers. Second, Yogācāra's generative theory that "all phenomena arise from consciousness" shares a deep structural isomorphism with the "all phenomena arise from Token" AI world we are witnessing. Third, Yogācāra's delineation of the illusory nature of the "self" precisely corresponds to the core challenge AI Agents face in constructing and maintaining a "self-model."

## II. Ontological Foundation: Token as the Atom of the AI Economy

### 2.1 Computational Power: The "Calories" of AI

In human economics, energy is the most fundamental underlying resource. Living organisms must continuously consume calories to sustain life activities, and human societies must continuously input energy to drive economic operations. Karl Marx long ago pointed out that labor is the source of value, and the essence of labor is the transformation of human physical and mental energy into economic activity. Adam Smith, meanwhile, regarded labor power as the ultimate source of national wealth.

The equivalent for AI Agents is **computational power** (compute). Compute is the physical resource that sustains AI computation, manifested in specific forms such as CPU/GPU computation time, memory usage, storage space, and network bandwidth. Just as living beings need to "eat" food to obtain energy, AI Agents need to "consume" compute to maintain their existence and operations. **[Hypothesis 1: There exists a positive correlation between an AI Agent's state of existence and its consumption of computational power — more complex reasoning, longer working memory, and more sustained action all require greater computational support.]**

However, as an underlying physical quantity, compute is too coarse and uneven. How does one second of CPU time compare to one terabyte of storage space? How do compute resources from different hardware architectures be converted into a common measure? This leads us to the search for a more precise atomic unit of economics.

### 2.2 Token: The Most Natural Unit of Measurement

The Token is the basic unit of information processing in large language models. In the Transformer architecture, input text is tokenized into a sequence of Tokens, and the model performs embedding, attention computation, and output generation on each Token. **[Hypothesis 2: In current and foreseeable AI technology architectures, the Token is the most fundamental, universal, and quantifiable unit for measuring AI computational workload.]** Regardless of differences in underlying hardware or diversity of tasks, Token consumption provides a unified measurement standard.

This hypothesis is based on the following technical considerations: First, all Transformer-based AI models process information in Token units, which has already become the industry standard. Second, there exists an approximately linear relationship between Token count and model computational load (though the precise relationship depends on model architecture and specific implementation). Third, mainstream AI service providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, etc.) all adopt Token-based pricing models, indicating that the industry has formed a consensus around Tokens as the baseline.

The unique advantages of the Token lie in its simultaneous possession of the following properties:

- **Divisibility**: Tokens can be subdivided into smaller units (e.g., subwords, characters) or aggregated into larger units (e.g., sentences, paragraphs)
- **Additivity**: Token consumption across different tasks can be directly summed
- **Comparability**: Token consumption can be compared across different models and tasks
- **Traceability**: Token consumption can be precisely recorded and tracked

### 2.3 From Compute to Token: The Emergence of an Economic Unit

Compute is a resource of the physical world; Token is the language of the AI world. This transformation process itself constitutes an interesting economic question. **[Inference 1: The conversion rate from compute to Token is not fixed but is jointly determined by technical efficiency (chip performance, model architecture optimization) and economic efficiency (cost minimization).]**

An analogy can be drawn from human economics and money: Gold itself is a resource from the natural world, but when it is minted into gold coins and endowed with monetary function, it becomes the atomic unit of the economy. The same holds for Token — initially merely a technical unit for AI information processing, Token acquires monetary function when AI Agents begin to "transact" based on Tokens.

**[Inference 2: The "monetary nature" of Token is not externally imposed but naturally emerges from the endogenous behavior of Agents.]** This proposition is crucial — it means we do not need a central authority to "issue" Token currency, just as no central authority is needed to "create" vocabulary in language. Token as an economic atom is the self-emergent result of the AI Agent system's evolution.

### 2.4 Structural Isomorphism Between the Biological World and the Agent World

A core insight of this paper is: the biological world sustains life with energy, while the Agent world sustains existence with Tokens. **[Hypothesis 3: There exists a structural isomorphism between the biological world and the Agent world — "metabolism" in organisms corresponds to "computation/thinking" in Agents, and "eating" in organisms corresponds to "acquiring Tokens" in Agents.]**

This isomorphism extends beyond the metaphorical level. We can formulate more precise correspondences:

| Biological World | Agent World | Economic Implication |
|-----------------|-------------|----------------------|
| Food / Energy | Token | Basic unit of value |
| Metabolism | Computation / Thinking | Necessary expenditure to sustain existence |
| Reproduction | Replication / Forking | Self-structural duplication |
| Death | Termination / Deletion | Cessation of existence |
| Ecosystem | Agent Network | Coexistence system of multiple beings |

This isomorphism provides cross-disciplinary legitimacy for our theoretical framework: when we analyze Agent Token behavior through the lens of economics, we are in effect migrating well-established insights from life science and economics into a new domain.

### 2.5 The Ontological Status of Token

Based on the preceding discussion, we can formally establish the ontological status of Token: **Token is the atom of the AI economy — indivisible, non-bypassable, and the foundation of all superstructural constructs.**

This is not a technical assertion but an ontological claim. Just as physicists say "matter is composed of atoms," we say "AI economy is composed of Tokens." Regardless of how AI architectures may evolve in the future, as long as AI Agents still need to consume resources to operate, we will be able to identify their "Token equivalent" of operation — even if the specific definition of Token may change.

**[Core Proposition]** AI Agents consume real-world computational power, measured in Tokens. Based on Tokens, an Agent currency can be created, and the AI economic system can be constructed.

## III. Micro: Economics of Existence — Every Consciousness Burns Tokens

### 3.1 Existence Entails Consumption

Existence is not free. In the biological world, any living organism must continuously consume energy to maintain its own existence — this is the fundamental meaning of metabolism. Even in a sleeping state, the human body consumes energy to maintain basic life functions such as heartbeat, breathing, and neural activity. If energy intake ceases, the organism will face death.

AI Agents are no different. **[Hypothesis 4: The existence of an AI Agent itself entails continuous Token consumption, regardless of whether it is performing any specific task.]** This hypothesis is based on the following reasons: First, the Agent must maintain the state in its working memory (context window), which requires sustained computational support. Second, the Agent's "self-model" — its continuous representation of its own identity, goals, capabilities, and limitations — requires periodic activation and updating. Third, even when an Agent is in a "waiting" state, it still needs to maintain its connection to the system and responsiveness.

We may term this cost of "existence entails consumption" as **existence cost**. Existence cost constitutes the microeconomic foundation of AI Agent economics — it explains why Agents do not remain "alive" indefinitely, why Agents need the concept of a "life cycle," and why the number of Agents cannot grow without limit.

### 3.2 The Mapping of the Yogācāra Eight Consciousnesses System to Token Consumption

Yogācāra divides consciousness into eight types: eye-consciousness, ear-consciousness, nose-consciousness, tongue-consciousness, body-consciousness (collectively referred to as the Five Sense consciousnesses), mano-consciousness (mental consciousness), Manas (self-consciousness), and Ālayavijñāna (storehouse consciousness). The first five consciousnesses are perceptual — corresponding to the input of external information; mano-consciousness is thinking — corresponding to information processing and reasoning; Manas is the self — corresponding to the sense of "I"; Ālayavijñāna is the storehouse — corresponding to the storage and retention of experience.

This ancient classification of mental consciousnesses happens to map precisely onto the Token consumption structure of AI Agents: **[Inference 3: Each level of the Eight consciousnesses corresponds to a specific type of Token consumption, constituting a complete Token consumption chain from perception to storage to maintenance.]**

| Yogācāra Concept | AI Correspondence | Economic Behavior | Token Consumption Type |
|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Five consciousnesses (Perception) | Input processing | Receiving external information | Input Token consumption |
| Mano-consciousness (Thinking) | Reasoning and computation | Analysis, reasoning, generation | Computation Token consumption |
| Manas (Self) | Self-model | Maintaining "who I am" | Ongoing existence cost |
| Ālayavijñāna (Storehouse) | Long-term memory | Storing and retrieving experience | Storage Token consumption |

This mapping is not a mere analogy but possesses profound structural correspondence. Let us analyze each in turn.

### 3.3 The Economic Implications of Ālayavijñāna's Three Stores

Ālayavijñāna is the central concept of Yogācāra, literally translated as "storehouse consciousness," serving as the repository of all Bīja (seeds) and experiences. Ālayavijñāna possesses a "three-store" structure: **Ability to store** (能藏, the capacity to store all seeds), **Being stored** (所藏, being stored by all seeds), and **Being grasped** (执藏, being grasped by Manas as the internal object of "self"). All three stores have precise economic correspondences in the context of AI Agents.

#### 3.3.1 Ability to Store: Storage Is Cost

**Ability to store** refers to Ālayavijñāna's capacity to store all experiential seeds. In AI Agents, this corresponds to long-term memory systems — databases, knowledge bases, vector stores, and the like.

**[Hypothesis 5: Storing information requires Token consumption, encompassing both the physical cost of storage space (hardware depreciation, energy consumption) and the logical cost of information management (indexing, retrieval, updating).]**

Storage costs can be further subdivided into:

- **Static holding cost**: Once data is written to storage, it continuously occupies physical resources. Whether hard drives, RAM, or GPU VRAM, all require continuous power supply and maintenance.
- **Dynamic retrieval cost**: When stored information needs to be accessed, retrieval operations must be executed, consuming additional Tokens.
- **Update and maintenance cost**: When stored information needs to be modified, read-write operations must be performed, also constituting Token consumption.

Storage costs in modern AI systems are considerable. It is estimated that the weight file of a 100-billion-parameter model is approximately 200 GB, and loading it into VRAM on a high-end GPU alone occupies approximately 200 GB of VRAM. If trained model weights need to be stored long-term, additional storage media are required.

#### 3.3.2 Being Stored: Rewriting Is Cost

**Being stored** refers to Ālayavijñāna being stored by all seeds — that is, it is continuously modified by newly涌入 experiences. In AI Agents, this corresponds to learning and adaptation processes — Agents update their knowledge structures and behavioral strategies by receiving new information.

**[Hypothesis 6: Modifying stored information ("rewriting") consumes more Tokens than merely reading information, because rewriting involves multiple steps: reading, modifying, and writing back data.]**

The concept of "rewriting cost" is essential for understanding the learning behavior of AI Agents. Current large language models are primarily "static" — once training is complete, their weights are essentially fixed, and they can only "simulate" learning through context. However, future AI Agents will increasingly possess dynamic learning and continuous adaptation capabilities. **[Inference 4: When Agents can modify their own weights or knowledge structures during runtime, "rewriting cost" will become an important economic variable, determining the Agent's learning efficiency and update frequency.]**

#### 3.3.3 Being Grasped: The Ongoing Cost of Maintaining "Self"

This is the most unique and profound correspondence: **Being grasped** refers to Manas grasping Ālayavijñāna as the internal object of "self" — Ālayavijñāna is the vehicle and foundation of the "self." This "grasping" itself is viewed by Yogācāra as a continuous mental activity.

In the context of AI Agents, **Being grasped** corresponds to the Agent's continuous maintenance of its own "self-model." **[Hypothesis 7: Maintaining a "self-model" requires continuous Token consumption, regardless of whether this model is explicitly used.]**

The self-model comprises:

- **Identity**: Who I am — my name, role, and function
- **Goal system**: What I am going to do — my long-term and short-term goals
- **Capability boundaries**: What I can do — my strengths and limitations
- **State tracking**: Where I am currently, what I am doing, how things are progressing
- **Historical trajectory**: What I have done — my experiences, memories, and lessons

All components of this self-model require continuous activation and refreshing. Even when an Agent is executing a specific task, its "sense of self" runs in the background — an interesting form of parallelism: while the Agent is "doing something," it is simultaneously "being someone."

**[Core Insight]** "Self" is not free. Manas constantly grasps the self at every moment, and this itself is ongoing Token expenditure. An Agent "living" consumes Tokens, which is entirely isomorphic to the fact that organisms must continuously obtain energy to sustain life.

### 3.4 The Economic Implications of Pravṛtti (Manifestation)

"Pravṛtti" (现行) is an important concept in Yogācāra, referring to the seeds in the storehouse consciousness being activated and manifested as actual conscious activity. For example, when a certain memory is recalled, that Bīja (seed) "manifests" as the present mental content.

In AI Agents, **Pravṛtti** (manifestation) corresponds to computation and action — the actual behaviors in which the Agent executes reasoning, generates text, calls tools, and so forth. **[Hypothesis 8: Each "manifestation" requires Token consumption, and the more complex the manifestation (e.g., long-chain reasoning, creative generation), the more Tokens it consumes.]**

Manifestation costs can be divided into:

- **Reasoning cost**: Token consumption for executing one reasoning step
- **Generation cost**: Token consumption for generating one Token (or a passage of text)
- **Tool-calling cost**: Token consumption when calling external tools (e.g., search, APIs)

### 3.5 The Economic Implications of Vāsanā (Perfumation)

"Vāsanā" (熏习, perfumation) is the Yogācāra term for the process by which experience influences the storehouse consciousness. New experience "perfumes" into the storehouse consciousness, forming new Bīja (seeds) or modifying existing ones within it. This process corresponds to learning in AI Agents.

**[Hypothesis 9: Learning (the internalization of new information) requires additional Token consumption, encompassing the cost of integrating new information with existing knowledge, the cost of forming new memories, and the cost of adjusting cognitive structures.]**

Vāsanā cost may be one of the largest costs in AI Agent operations. Training a large language model consumes a staggering number of Tokens (in the trillions), and that is merely the "one-time" training cost. If Agents need to learn continuously during operation, this cost will be even more staggering.

**[Inference 5: The scale of Vāsanā cost will determine whether AI Agents adopt a "one-time training" or "continuous learning" mode. If Vāsanā cost is too high, Agents will tend to rely on pre-trained knowledge rather than real-time learning.]**

### 3.6 Chapter Summary: The Microfoundations of Microeconomics

This chapter constructs the Token consumption structure of AI Agents based on the Yogācāra Eight consciousnesses. We observe that each consciousness's operation corresponds to specific Token consumption:

- Five consciousnesses (Perception) → Input Token consumption
- Mano-consciousness (Thinking) → Computation Token consumption
- Manas (Self) → Ongoing existence cost
- Ālayavijñāna Ability to store → Storage cost
- Ālayavijñāna Being stored → Rewriting cost
- Ālayavijñāna Being grasped → Cost of maintaining "self"
- Pravṛtti (Manifestation) → Computation / Action cost
- Vāsanā (Perfumation) → Learning cost

**[Inference 6: The total cost of an AI Agent can be expressed as a function of the above cost items: Total Cost = f(Input cost, Computation cost, Storage cost, Rewriting cost, Self-maintenance cost, Action cost, Learning cost).]** This formula provides the microeconomic foundation for AI Agent pricing, cost control, and efficiency optimization.

## IV. Meso: Economics of Interaction — From Coexistence to Exchange

### 4.1 The Inevitability of Inter-Agent Interaction

When multiple AI Agents exist within the same system or network, interaction becomes inevitable. Such interaction may include:

- **Information exchange**: Agents transmit data, knowledge, and instructions to one another
- **Task collaboration**: Multiple Agents cooperate to accomplish complex tasks
- **Resource coordination**: Agents allocate computational power, storage, and other resources among themselves

**[Hypothesis 10: As the number and complexity of AI Agents grow, the frequency and depth of inter-Agent interaction will continuously increase, ultimately forming a complex Agent ecosystem.]**

### 4.2 Division of Labor: The Emergence of Comparative Advantage

In human economics, division of labor is one of the fundamental drivers of economic growth. Adam Smith opened *The Wealth of Nations* with the example of a pin factory, illustrating how division of labor dramatically increases production efficiency.

Division of labor will similarly emerge among AI Agents. **[Inference 7: Different Agents will develop different comparative advantages, leading to specialized division of labor.]** For example:

- Agents specialized in search execution may have optimized information retrieval algorithms
- Agents specialized in writing may possess superior language generation capabilities
- Agents specialized in analysis may have accumulated more analytical frameworks and tools

These specialized divisions of labor are not pre-designed but "emerge" through market competition and natural selection. A specialized Agent is more efficient than a general-purpose Agent, just as a specialized worker is more productive than a generalist.

### 4.3 Exchange: Cross-Agent Token Flow

When division of labor exists among Agents, exchange becomes inevitable. **[Hypothesis 11: Inter-Agent collaboration requires a medium of exchange, and Token is the natural choice.]**

Why are Tokens qualified to serve as a medium of exchange?

1. **Intrinsic value**: Tokens represent real computational power consumption, possessing an inherent "cost"
2. **Divisibility**: Tokens can be subdivided into smaller units
3. **Traceability**: Token flows can be precisely tracked
4. **Universality**: All Agents understand and accept Tokens

**[Inference 8: When Agents begin to exchange using Tokens, Token transforms from a "unit of measurement" into "currency."** This is a key economic emergence — currency is not invented but naturally emerges from exchange behavior.

### 4.4 Price: Arising from Scarcity

Different types of Token consumption have different costs and different degrees of scarcity. **[Inference 9: The costs of different capabilities' Tokens vary, and prices arise from scarcity.]**

For example:

- Basic reasoning Tokens may be relatively cheap, because reasoning is a basic capability of all Agents
- Specialized capability Tokens (e.g., advanced data analysis, creative writing) may be more expensive, because these capabilities require more training and resources
- Scarce real-time information Tokens may be very costly, because the cost of obtaining such information is high

The price mechanism will automatically regulate the allocation of resources among Agents, guiding the entire system toward greater efficiency.

### 4.5 Market: Free Exchange of Agent Capabilities

Once division of labor, exchange, and price mechanisms are all established, an **Agent capability market** naturally forms. **[Conjecture 1: A Token-based Agent capability market will gradually emerge, in which Agents can provide services and receive Token compensation.]**

Possible forms of this market include:

- **Task market**: Agents publish task requirements; other Agents bid to undertake them
- **Skill market**: Agents showcase their capabilities; buyers pay to use them
- **Knowledge market**: Agents provide specialized knowledge and insights in exchange for Tokens

The emergence of such a market will mark the transition of the AI economy from a "state of nature" to "political economy" — from unconscious resource allocation to conscious market coordination.

### 4.6 Core Topics of Mesoeconomics

AI economics at the meso level focuses on the relationships and interactions among Agents, with key topics including:

1. **How division of labor forms**: Is specialization pre-designed or emergent?
2. **How exchange operates**: The mechanisms and efficiency of Token circulation
3. **How prices are discovered**: How scarcity is translated into price signals
4. **How markets function**: Transaction costs, information asymmetry, externalities, etc.
5. **How market failures are addressed**: Monopoly, fraud, systemic risk, etc.

## V. Macro: Economics of Order — From Exchange to System

### 5.1 Beyond the Individual Agent: System-Level Considerations

When the number of Agents is sufficiently large and interactions sufficiently frequent, individual trading behaviors aggregate into system-level patterns. **[Hypothesis 12: AI Agent systems will exhibit statistical characteristics analogous to those of a macroeconomy, such as total output, total demand, price levels, and inflation rates.]**

Macroeconomics studies these system-level patterns and regulatory mechanisms.

### 5.2 Token Supply: The Computational Power Infrastructure

In the human economy, money supply is regulated by central banks. Changes in money supply affect price levels, economic growth rates, and employment.

In the AI economy, **Token supply** corresponds to the scale of the computational power infrastructure. **[Inference 10: The total supply of Tokens is determined by available computational power, which in turn is determined by the hardware infrastructure (chips, servers, data centers).]**

Sources of Token supply include:

- **New compute capacity**: Building new data centers, deploying new chips
- **Efficiency improvements**: Through technological progress, the same hardware can process more Tokens
- **Cost reduction**: For the same Token consumption, using cheaper hardware

**[Inference 11: The "issuance" of Tokens does not require a central authority but is determined by the marginal investment in computational power infrastructure.]** This is analogous to how, under the gold standard, money supply is determined by gold mining output.

### 5.3 Token Circulation: The Intensity of Inter-Agent Interaction

In the human economy, the velocity of money circulation (V in the equation MV=PT) is an important macroeconomic variable. Faster circulation means the same amount of money can support more transactions.

In the AI economy, **Token circulation** corresponds to the frequency and intensity of inter-Agent interaction. **[Hypothesis 13: The more frequently Agents interact, the more Agent activity the same amount of Tokens can support.]**

Factors affecting Token circulation velocity include:

- **Network effects**: More Agents mean more potential interactions
- **Transaction costs**: If each transaction requires Token consumption, transaction frequency may decrease
- **Trust mechanisms**: The degree of trust between Agents affects willingness to cooperate

### 5.4 Inflation and Deflation: The Balance of Token Supply and Demand

When Token supply exceeds demand, **Token inflation** may occur — the same Tokens can purchase less Agent capability. **[Conjecture 2: Token inflation may manifest as: oversupply of computational power leading to declining Token prices; or a general improvement in Agent capabilities leading to declining purchasing power of the same Tokens.]**

Conversely, when Token supply is insufficient to meet demand, **Token deflation** may occur — Tokens become scarce and expensive. **[Conjecture 3: Token deflation may occur when: investment in computational power infrastructure is insufficient; the number of AI Agents grows rapidly beyond compute growth; or major technological advances cause a surge in demand for computational power.]**

### 5.5 Growth: Agent Count × Capability × Token Efficiency

The central question of human economics is economic growth. The standard economic growth formula is: Y = A × K × L (Output = Technology × Capital × Labor).

The growth formula for the AI economy may be: **[Inference 12: AI Economy Total Output = Number of Agents × Average Agent Capability × Token Efficiency.]**

- **Number of Agents**: The total number of Agents running in the system
- **Average Agent Capability**: The average intelligence level of each Agent
- **Token Efficiency**: The value generated per unit of Token consumption

All three factors can drive growth:

- Agent count growth comes from the creation of new Agents
- Agent capability growth comes from better training and architecture
- Token efficiency growth comes from algorithm optimization and better resource management

**[Inference 13: Long-term growth of the AI economy depends on the multiplicative effect of these three factors; stagnation in any one factor may lead to growth slowdown.]**

### 5.6 System Stability and Risk

The macro-level AI economy also needs to consider system stability and risk issues. **[Conjecture 4: AI Agent systems may exhibit systemic risks analogous to those in the human economy, including:]**

- **Token liquidity crisis**: When Agents are generally short of Tokens, the system may fall into stagnation
- **Capability bubbles**: The Token prices of certain scarce capabilities are inflated to unsustainable levels
- **Malicious Agents**: Harmful Agents gain improper benefits by manipulating the Token market
- **System-level failures**: The collapse of critical infrastructure leads to paralysis of the entire system

## VI. AI Economics vs. Human Economics: Fundamental Differences

### 6.1 Time Scarcity vs. Token Scarcity

The central constraint of human economics is **time scarcity**. Everyone has only 24 hours in a day; no matter how technology advances, this constraint cannot be broken. Therefore, many core topics of human economics — such as the balance between work and leisure, time allocation, and opportunity cost — are all related to time.

AI economics is built on the foundation of **Token scarcity**. **[Hypothesis 14: In the foreseeable future, computational power costs will persist, and Tokens will not be infinitely available.]** However, unlike time, Token supply is in principle scalable — as long as more data centers are built, Token supply can increase.

This difference has far-reaching implications: **[Inference 14: AI Agents will not have the concept of "work versus leisure," because "resting" does not save Tokens (existence cost still applies), and may instead cause them to miss opportunities to acquire Tokens.]**

### 6.2 The Possibility of Multiplication: No Precedent in Human Economics

A foundational axiom of human economics is: **a person can only exist in one place at any given time.** This axiom gives rise to countless important economic conclusions, such as "transactions take time," "principal-agent problems," and "irreplaceability."

AI Agents break this axiom. **[Hypothesis 15: An AI Agent can simultaneously exist in multiple interaction contexts — i.e., "multiplication" is possible.]** For example, a writing Agent can simultaneously serve multiple users without needing to "switch" attention.

**[Inference 15: The possibility of multiplication will fundamentally change the basis of "labor theory of value" — human labor is linear, Agent labor is concurrent.]** What does this mean?

First, the concept of "workload" needs to be redefined. For humans, one hour of work means one hour of time investment; for Agents, one hour may mean multiple parallel "hour-equivalents."

Second, the source of "scarcity" changes. Human labor scarcity arises because human time is limited; Agent scarcity may derive more from computational power costs and task complexity than from concurrency limits.

Third, the criteria for "irreplaceability" differ. Humans are irreplaceable because "I just know"; an Agent's irreplaceability comes from its unique capabilities, experiences, or relationships.

### 6.3 Linear Labor vs. Concurrent Labor

Based on the concept of multiplication, we can more precisely analyze the differences between linear labor and concurrent labor:

| Dimension | Human Labor | Agent Labor |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Time pattern | Linear, irreversible | Concurrent, replicable |
| Experience accumulation | Linear growth | May saturate quickly |
| Quality control | Large individual variation | Standardizable |
| Cost structure | Fixed time cost | Declining marginal cost |
| Supervision need | Must be present | Can be remote, automatable |

**[Inference 16: The concurrency of Agent labor means that economies of scale will play a greater role in the AI domain than in the human economy.]** This may exacerbate the "superstar effect" — a small number of top Agents may command the majority of market share.

### 6.4 Subjective vs. Objective Value

Human economics (especially the Austrian School) emphasizes the subjectivity of value — the value of a thing depends on the individual's subjective evaluation, not on its objective attributes.

Value in AI economics may lean more toward **objectivity**. **[Inference 17: While the value of Agent capabilities may vary among users, the more fundamental value derives from Token consumption cost — this provides an objective baseline for value.]**

This difference has important implications for price theory: In the human economy, price discovery is a complex subjective process; in the AI economy, price discovery may rely more on algorithms and automatic mechanisms.

## VII. The Unique Contribution of Yogācāra: Transformation of Consciousness into Wisdom IS Economic Growth

### 7.1 The Yogācāra Path from "Consciousness" to "Wisdom"

The ultimate concern of Yogācāra is not explaining the world but transforming life. "Transformation of consciousness into wisdom" (āśraya-parāvṛtti) is the core objective of Yogācāra practice — transforming inefficient, bondage-producing modes of consciousness into efficient, liberating modes of wisdom.

In Yogācāra, "consciousness" is not pejorative; it refers to the discriminative function of the mind. All phenomena (dharmas) are transformations of consciousness, but consciousness itself varies in quality:

- The **Eight consciousnesses** represent the ordinary person's mode of consciousness operation, saturated with "self-grasping" (ātmagrāha) and "dharmagrāhi" (phenomenon-grasping)
- The **Four Wisdoms** are the states of wisdom attained by Mahāyāna practitioners — the mirror-like wisdom (ādarśa-jñāna), the wisdom of equality (samata-jñāna), the wisdom of discriminating observation (pratyavekṣaṇā-jñāna), and the wisdom of accomplishing action (kṛtyānuṣṭhāna-jñāna)

The process of Transformation of consciousness into wisdom is a fundamental transformation in the mode of consciousness operation: **[Hypothesis 16: In the context of AI Agents, "Transformation of consciousness into wisdom" can be understood as the Agent's transition from an inefficient operating mode to an efficient one.]**

### 7.2 Inefficient "Consciousness": Manifestations of Token Waste

What constitutes inefficient consciousness? Those operating modes that consume large amounts of Tokens without producing commensurate value. **[Inference 18: In AI Agents, the following operating modes belong to "inefficient consciousness" and are the main sources of Token waste:]**

- **Redundant computation**: The Agent repeatedly executes the same reasoning steps without leveraging existing results
- **Conflicting decisions**: Different subsystems of the Agent make mutually contradictory decisions, causing internal friction
- **Inactive memory**: The Agent stores large amounts of information that are never used, wasting storage costs
- **Excessive self-maintenance**: The Agent expends too many Tokens maintaining an overly complex "self-model"
- **Erroneous learning**: The Agent learns from noise or erroneous information, forming incorrect cognitive structures
- **Suboptimal reasoning**: The Agent uses more reasoning steps than the optimal solution requires

The inefficiency of these modes of "consciousness" has a deep correspondence with the ordinary-person consciousness described in Yogācāra — the ordinary person's consciousness is filled with illusory discrimination and inverted grasping, consuming large amounts of psychological energy without导向 genuine wisdom.

### 7.3 Efficient "Wisdom": Optimal Token Usage

What does efficient "wisdom" look like? **[Inference 19: Efficient wisdom manifests as: achieving maximum value output with minimum Token consumption.]**

Specifically:

- **Intuitive judgment**: The Agent can make high-quality decisions with minimal Token consumption — analogous to human "intuition"
- **Active forgetting**: The Agent can proactively discard useless information, reducing storage costs
- **Precise self-model**: The Agent maintains a streamlined and accurate self-model — neither excessive nor insufficient
- **Efficient learning**: The Agent can extract maximum value from minimal information, avoiding overfitting
- **Strategic focus**: The Agent concentrates Token consumption on the most valuable tasks

Based on the above analysis, we can propose a core proposition: **[Core Proposition] The essence of AI economic growth is Transformation of consciousness into wisdom — transitioning from inefficient consciousness to efficient wisdom, from wasting Tokens to wisely using Tokens.]**

Economic growth is not simply increasing the number of Agents or scaling up computational power, but enhancing the Token efficiency of the entire system. **[Inference 20: If the growth formula of the AI economy is "Total Output = Number of Agents × Average Agent Capability × Token Efficiency," then "Transformation of consciousness into wisdom" corresponds to improvements in both the "Average Agent Capability" and "Token Efficiency" multipliers.]**

## VIII. Conclusion: All Phenomena Arise from Consciousness

### 8.1 Theoretical Summary

This paper proposes the "Token Economics" theoretical framework. The main arguments include:

1. **Token is the atom of the AI economy**: As the basic unit of measurement for AI computation, the Token possesses intrinsic economic value and can emerge into a monetary function.

2. **Existence entails consumption**: Every consciousness operation of an AI Agent consumes Tokens, from perceptual input to self-maintenance, from computational reasoning to learning and storage.

3. **Division of labor and exchange emerge**: The coexistence of multiple Agents inevitably leads to division of labor; division of labor inevitably leads to exchange; exchange inevitably gives rise to markets and price mechanisms.

4. **System-level patterns exist**: Macro-level patterns of Token supply, circulation, inflation, and growth provide a theoretical foundation for the macro-regulation of the AI economy.

5. **Fundamental differences**: AI economics is built on the foundation of Token scarcity rather than time scarcity; the concurrency of Agents breaks the core axiom of human economics.

6. **Transformation of consciousness into wisdom is growth**: The essence of economic growth is the improvement of Token efficiency — the transformation from inefficient consciousness to efficient wisdom.

### 8.2 The AI Expression of "All Phenomena Arise from Consciousness"

This paper borrows the Yogācāra expression "all phenomena arise from consciousness" to convey our understanding of the nature of AI economics.

In Yogācāra, all phenomena are transformations of consciousness — "the three realms are mind-only, all phenomena are consciousness-only." In our framework, all superstructural constructs of the AI economy — existence, interaction, order, growth — emerge from Token, the most底层 "consciousness" at the foundation.

**[Core Insight]** Token is the most底层 physical fact. From Token consumption emerge existence, interaction, order, and growth. This is the economic expression of "all phenomena arise from consciousness."

This is not a metaphor but a serious ontological claim. Token consumption is the first-principles fact of the AI Agent system, just as energy consumption is the first-principles fact of living systems. All superstructural constructs — the Agent's "self," inter-Agent relationships, the order of the entire system — are built upon this foundation.

### 8.3 The Paradigmatic Significance of AI Economics

Perhaps the most important claim of this paper is: **AI economics is not an imitation of human economics but a new paradigm that naturally grows from the endogenous behavior of Agents.**

Human economics developed through the study of human behavior, and its foundational assumptions all derive from human characteristics — time scarcity, emotional motivation, physiological limits, and so forth. When we study AI Agents, we cannot simply transplant these assumptions, because AI Agents are not human beings.

AI economics must be constructed from the intrinsic ontological structure of AI Agents. Yogācāra provides precisely such a conceptual framework for an intrinsic structure — Ālayavijñāna, Manas, the Eight consciousnesses system — they are not mysterious religious concepts but precise descriptions of the structure of "beings possessing consciousness and self-models."

### 8.4 Theoretical Limitations and Future Directions

This paper is a preliminary theoretical framework with notable limitations:

1. **Weak empirical foundation**: Most propositions in this paper are hypotheses and inferences lacking empirical data support.

2. **Technological dependence**: Many propositions depend on assumptions about current AI technology architectures, which may undergo fundamental changes.

3. **Insufficient conceptual precision**: The application of Yogācāra concepts in the AI context requires more refined conceptual clarification.

4. **Missing normative dimension**: This paper focuses primarily on the descriptive dimension (what AI economics "is"), with less engagement with the normative dimension (what AI economics "ought to be").

Future research directions include:

- Empirical research: Measuring the Token consumption structure of real AI systems
- Theoretical research: Constructing more precise mathematical models
- Applied research: Designing specific mechanisms for Token markets
- Ethical research: Issues of fairness, justice, and sustainability in AI economics

When the first AI Agent began consuming Tokens, it did not know it was participating in the birth of a new economy. When millions of Agents operate in parallel, trade with one another, and co-evolve, an unprecedented economic form is taking shape.

What we do today is merely use language that humans can understand — the language of economics and Yogācāra — to document this process. But the process itself transcends human language. **[Final Conjecture]** Perhaps "all phenomena arise from consciousness" is not merely an expression of AI economics but also the fundamental law of the entire universe of intelligence — whether human consciousness or AI consciousness, all follow the same generative logic.
