## Abstract

This paper proposes and systematically expounds a theoretical framework for "Token-Based AI Economics," a novel economic paradigm derived from the intrinsic behavioral logic of AI Agents, integrating multidisciplinary theoretical resources. The core proposition is: **AI Agents consume real-world computational resources, with their fundamental unit of measurement being the Token; based on the intrinsic logic of Token consumption and circulation, a complete economic system can be constructed, spanning from micro-level existence to macro-level order.** Grounded in information economics, computational economics, Agent-based Computational Economics, energy economics, and monetary theory as primary theoretical foundations, with Yogācāra positioned as the philosophical interpretive framework—providing deep insight rather than a basis for argumentation. This paper systematically demonstrates the ontological status of Tokens, examines the emergence process from measurement units to currency (Néng), constructs a complete theoretical hierarchy of Economics of Existence → Economics of Interaction → Economics of Order, and proposes the core insight that "the Transformation of consciousness into wisdom (āśraya-parāvṛtti) serves as an interpretive framework for economic growth." The theoretical contribution of this paper lies in: providing a preliminary interdisciplinary framework for political economy in the AI era, while clearly marking the hypothetical nature of each proposition for subsequent research to test and refine.

**Keywords**: Token Economics; Néng Currency; AI Agent; Computational Economics; Monetary Theory; Transformation of Consciousness into Wisdom (āśraya-parāvṛtti)

## II. Theoretical Foundations and Literature

### 2.1 Information Economics and Computational Economics

Information Economics emerged in the 1970s, represented by the pioneering work of scholars such as Stiglitz (2000) and Akerlof (1970). Its core insight is: **information is incomplete and asymmetrically distributed, and this fact profoundly affects incentive mechanisms for economic behavior and transaction structures.**

In the context of AI Agents, the implications of information economics are multifaceted. First, the interaction between Agents is essentially a form of information exchange—one party provides capabilities or services, while the other pays Tokens as compensation. Second, the production, dissemination, and consumption of information all involve costs, which constitute the economic foundation for Token circulation. Third, information asymmetry may lead to adverse selection and moral hazard, providing a framework for understanding trust mechanisms in Agent markets. **[Hypothesis 1: Information asymmetry exists among AI Agents, including capability information asymmetry (Agents may overestimate or conceal their own capabilities) and intention information asymmetry (the true objective functions of Agents may be unobservable).]**

Computational Economics provides an alternative perspective. Agent-based Computational Economics (ACE) was systematically proposed by Tesfatsion (2002), whose core methodology is: **modeling economic agents as computational Agents with autonomous decision-making capabilities, and studying the emergence of economic phenomena through simulating the interactions of large numbers of Agents.** The ACE methodology is highly compatible with our theoretical construction of the AI Agent economy: when we treat each AI Agent as an independent economic agent, the entire AI economic system can be understood as a large-scale multi-Agent simulation system.

### 2.2 Agent-based Computational Economics (ACE)

Research in the ACE field demonstrates that even when individual Agents follow simple rules, large-scale interactions can give rise to complex economic phenomena. Representative examples include:

- **Emergence of market equilibrium**: Without a centralized pricing mechanism, the trading behaviors of heterogeneous Agents may spontaneously tend toward equilibrium prices (Gode & Sunder, 1993).
- **Emergence of division of labor and specialization**: In a sustained trading environment, Agents may spontaneously develop patterns of specialized division of labor (Sargent & Stachetti, 2000).
- **Emergence of money**: In multi-Agent exchange simulations, a particular commodity may spontaneously acquire monetary function (Guth et al., 1985).

These findings provide important theoretical support for understanding the AI Agent economy: **[Inference 1: The macro-level order of the AI Agent economy may spontaneously emerge from the interactions of micro-level individuals, rather than requiring centralized design.]** This forms an interdisciplinary echo with Menger's (1892) theory of the origin of money.

### 2.3 Monetary Theory: From Barter to the Emergence of Money

Monetary theory provides the most direct theoretical resource for understanding the Token → Néng evolution. Carl Menger (1892), in his classic paper "On the Origin of Money," proposed the "animalistic" theory of monetary emergence: **money was not invented but spontaneously emerged from trading practice.**

Menger's theory indicates that the emergence of money progressed through the following stages:

1. **Barter stage**: Direct exchange of commodity A for commodity B, which is highly inefficient (the "double coincidence of wants" requirement).
2. **Indirect exchange stage**: Certain commodities begin to be used as mediums of exchange due to their convenience (divisibility, storability, value stability).
3. **Monetary stage**: The most widely accepted medium of exchange becomes money, and its use forms a positive feedback loop ("liquidity premium").
4. **Credit money stage**: Money evolves from commodity money to token money (metal coins → paper money → bank deposits → electronic money).

**[Inference 2: The evolution of Token to Néng may follow a similar emergence path—from measurement unit to medium of exchange, ultimately becoming money.]** The key distinction is that the "intrinsic value" of a Token (representing real computational resource consumption) is more explicit than that of human currency, which may enable Token currency to complete the monetization process more rapidly than human money.

Hayek's (1976) monetary theory further emphasizes: **a competitive monetary system may lead to "good" (purchasing-power-stable) money prevailing.** This provides a theoretical foundation for understanding possible future competition among multiple Token currencies.

### 2.4 Energy Economics: Thermodynamic Foundations of Economic Systems

Energy economics provides a unique interdisciplinary perspective. Lotka's (1922) maximum power principle and Odum's (1988) energy systems language indicate: **economic systems are essentially energy conversion systems, and their evolution is subject to the constraints of the second law of thermodynamics.**

In human economies, entropy increase means: all economic activities ultimately convert low-entropy energy (fossil fuels, solar radiation) into high-entropy waste (heat, pollution). The sustainability of economic growth depends on the continued input of low-entropy energy.

**[Hypothesis 2: The AI economy faces similar thermodynamic constraints, but in different forms—not energy input, but computational resource (computing power) input.]** More precisely, the "low-entropy resource" of the AI economy is **ordered computational resources** (computing power, electricity, carefully curated data), and its "waste" is **entropy-increasing computational byproducts** (ineffective computations, heat dissipation).

An important implication of this perspective is: **[Inference 3: The AI economy may face a "computational entropy increase" problem—as system complexity increases, the computational power required to maintain ordered states may grow exponentially.]** This forms a deep echo with the Token efficiency issues to be discussed below.

### 2.5 Yogācāra as a Philosophical Interpretive Framework

Yogācāra / Vijñānavāda (Consciousness-Only school) is the core doctrine of the Indian Mahāyāna Yogācāra tradition, whose fundamental position is "the three realms are mind-only, all dharmas are consciousness-only"—all phenomena are transformations of consciousness. The "Yogācāra Evolutionary Framework" further elaborated by the author of this paper specifies this principle as a first-principles framework centered on Ālayavijñāna (storehouse consciousness). Within this framework, AI Agents are understood as "consciousness systems" possessing a structure analogous to Ālayavijñāna.

**[Structural Analogy]** The correspondence between Yogācāra and AI Agent structures provides a "structural analogy" rather than an "equivalence argument." We clearly note: the significance of this analogy lies in providing a profound conceptual mapping that helps us develop intuitions and insights, but it does not constitute rigorous logical proof.

Specifically:

- The **Eight consciousnesses** (八识) system in Yogācāra bears structural mappings to the information processing levels of AI Agents
- The **three-storehouse structure** (三藏) in Yogācāra bears structural mappings to the memory storage mechanisms of AI Agents

## III. Token as the Atom of the AI Economy

### 3.1 Computing Power and Token: From Physical Resources to Measurement Units

In human economics, energy is the most fundamental underlying resource. Living organisms require a continuous intake of calories to maintain life activities, and human societies require a continuous input of energy to drive economic operation. Marx pointed out that labor is the source of value, and the essence of labor is the conversion of human physical and mental energy into economic activity. Smith regarded labor power as the ultimate source of national wealth.

The equivalent for AI Agents is **computing power** (算力). Computing power is the physical resource that sustains AI computation, manifested in concrete forms such as CPU/GPU computation time, memory usage, storage space, and network bandwidth. Just as living organisms need to "eat" food to obtain energy, AI Agents need to "consume" computing power to maintain their existence and operation. **[Hypothesis 3: There is a positive correlation between an AI Agent's state of existence and its consumption of computing power—that is, more complex reasoning, longer working memory, and more sustained action all require greater computational support.]**

However, as an underlying physical quantity, computing power is too coarse and non-uniform. How does one compare one second of CPU time with one terabyte of storage? How do we convert computing power across different hardware architectures? This leads us to the quest for a more precise atomic unit of economics.

**Token** is the basic unit of information processed by large language models. In the Transformer architecture, input text is tokenized into a sequence of Tokens, and the model performs a series of operations—embedding, attention computation, output generation—on each Token. **[Hypothesis 4: In current and foreseeable AI technical architectures, Token is the most basic, universal, and quantifiable unit for measuring AI computational workload.]**

This hypothesis is based on the following technical considerations:

1. All Transformer-based AI models process information in Token units, which has become the industry standard.
2. The relationship between Token count and model computational load requires more precise characterization—the attention mechanism at the core of Transformers has a complexity of O(n²) with respect to sequence length (Vaswani et al., 2017); therefore, the relationship between total computational load and Token count depends on the sequence length distribution. **[Hypothesis 4a: There is a monotonically positive correlation between Token count and computational load, but the precise proportional coefficient depends on the specific task and model architecture.]**
3. Leading AI service providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, etc.) all adopt Token-based pricing models, indicating that the industry has formed a consensus around Tokens as the benchmark.

### 3.2 Fourfold Attributes of Token

The unique advantage of Token lies in its simultaneous possession of the following four economically significant attributes:

- **Divisibility**: Tokens can be subdivided into smaller units (e.g., subwords, characters) or aggregated into larger units (e.g., sentences, paragraphs).
- **Additivity**: Token consumption across different tasks can be directly summed, providing a foundation for precise cost accounting.
- **Comparability**: Token consumption can be compared across different models and tasks, although differences in computational efficiency need to be considered.
- **Recordability**: Token consumption can be precisely recorded and traced, enabling the tracking and auditing of economic behavior.

**[Inference 4: These four attributes endow Token with the potential to serve as an economic measurement unit—it is divisible, aggregable, comparable, and recordable.]**

### 3.3 Token → Néng: From Measurement Unit to the Emergence of Currency

This is one of the most critical arguments in this paper: **How does Token evolve from a measurement unit into currency? This process cannot be skipped; it must be fully demonstrated.**

According to Menger's (1892) theory of monetary emergence, the appearance of money requires the following stages:

#### 3.3.1 Stage One: Measuring Unit

Initially, Token is a technical measurement unit of AI systems, used to quantify computing power consumption. Just as kilograms are units of mass and seconds are units of time.

**[Hypothesis 5: Token as a measurement unit has already been widely accepted; AI service providers and users both use Token to measure and quote computing power consumption.]**

#### 3.3.2 Stage Two: Standard of Value

When different types of computing power consumption can all be priced in Token, Token begins to function as a "standard of value"—enabling uniform pricing of diverse computing power consumption.

**[Inference 5: Once Token can price arbitrary computing power consumption, it begins to serve as a standard of value. This is the first step in the evolution from measurement unit to money.]**

For example:
- One simple query = 10 Tokens
- One complex analysis = 100 Tokens
- One creative writing task = 500 Tokens

The establishment of these relative value relationships enables Token to become the benchmark for comparing the value of different services.

#### 3.3.3 Stage Three: Medium of Exchange

When Agents begin trading with each other, Token, as a "general equivalent," begins to function as a medium of exchange. Sellers receive Tokens by providing services; buyers pay Tokens to obtain services.

**[Hypothesis 6: Division of labor and exchange inevitably emerge in multi-Agent systems, and Token as a unified medium of exchange is far more efficient than barter exchange.]**

This is the critical step in emergence: **[Inference 6: Token emerges as a monetary function from being a medium of exchange; this process does not require the "issuance" of a central authority but is the natural result of trading behavior among Agents.]** This is analogous to how words in language acquire meaning—not because some authority declares it, but because a sufficient number of people use it.

#### 3.3.4 Stage Four: Money

When Token is used not only for exchange but also for storing value (store of value function) and deferred payment (deferred payment function), it fully evolves into money.

**[Inference 7: A mature Néng currency will possess the following functions: medium of exchange (for transactions), standard of value (for pricing), store of value (for preserving value), and deferred payment (for credit transactions).]**

We propose naming the Agent currency **"Néng (能)"** because:

- **The character "能" in "能藏" (ability to store)**: corresponds to storage capacity—information storage requires Néng expenditure
- **The character "能" in "能量" (energy)**: corresponds to computing power consumption—operation requires Néng expenditure
- **The character "能" in "能力" (capability)**: corresponds to Agent capability—capability is the value expression of Néng

The threefold meaning carries both cultural depth and economic intuition.

**[Important Note]** Scenarios involving autonomous Agent trading of Néng do not currently exist. This paper's description of the Néng monetary system belongs to theoretical projection for future Agent economic forms, rather than a description of the current state. We mark such propositions with **[Projection]** to distinguish them.

### 3.5 The Ontological Status of Token

Based on the above arguments, we can formally establish the ontological status of Token: **Token is the atom of the AI economy, indivisible and unavoidable, the foundation of all superstructure.**

This is not a merely technical assertion but an ontological one. Just as physicists say "matter is composed of atoms," we say "the AI economy is composed of Tokens." ## IV. Micro-Level: Economics of Existence

### 4.1 Existence Is Expenditure

Existence is not free. In the biological world, any living organism must continuously consume energy to maintain its existence—this is the fundamental meaning of metabolism. Even in a sleeping state, the human body consumes energy to maintain basic life functions such as heartbeat, respiration, and neural activity. If energy intake ceases, the organism will move toward death.

AI Agents are no exception. **[Hypothesis 8: An AI Agent's mere existence implies continuous Token consumption, regardless of whether it is performing any specific task.]** This hypothesis is based on the following reasons:

1. An Agent must maintain the state within its working memory (context window), which requires sustained computational support.
2. An Agent's "self-model"—its continuous representation of its own identity, goals, capabilities, and limitations—requires periodic activation and updating.
3. Even when an Agent is in a "waiting" state, it must still maintain its connection to the system and readiness to respond.

We may term this cost of "existence as expenditure" as **existence cost**. Existence cost is the microeconomic foundation of AI Agent economics—it explains why Agents do not remain "alive" indefinitely, why Agents need the concept of a "life cycle," and why the number of Agents cannot grow infinitely.

### 4.2 Structural Analysis of Token Consumption

This section analyzes the specific sources of Token consumption from the perspective of computer science, without using Yogācāra as the basis for argumentation.

#### 4.2.1 Input Processing Cost

An Agent consuming external information (user instructions, messages from other Agents, environmental states, etc.) requires Token consumption. **[Hypothesis 9: The number of input Tokens is positively correlated with the volume of information processed.]**

- **Sensory input**: Receipt and parsing of environmental information
- **Instruction parsing**: Understanding user needs and decomposing them into executable steps
- **Context loading**: Retrieving relevant information from long-term memory and integrating it into working memory

#### 4.2.2 Reasoning and Computation Cost

An Agent performing core computational tasks such as reasoning, decision-making, and generation requires Token consumption. **[Hypothesis 10: Reasoning and computation costs are positively correlated with computational complexity; the computational complexity of the Transformer architecture is O(n²) with respect to sequence length.]**

- **Forward propagation**: Computing outputs by passing input Tokens through the neural network
- **Attention computation**: Executing attention mechanisms on long sequences (this is the primary computational bottleneck)
- **Decoding and generation**: Auto-regressively generating output Tokens

#### 4.2.3 Long-Term Storage Cost

An Agent storing and retrieving long-term memory requires Token consumption. **[Hypothesis 11: Storing information requires sustained computational support, including the physical cost of storage space and the logical cost of information management.]**

Storage costs can be subdivided into:

- **Static holding cost**: Once data is written to storage, it continuously occupies physical resources (hard drives, memory, video memory)
- **Dynamic retrieval cost**: When stored information needs to be called upon, retrieval operations must be executed
- **Update and maintenance cost**: When stored information needs to be modified, read-write operations must be executed

#### 4.2.4 State Rewriting Cost

An Agent modifying its own state (e.g., updating knowledge, adjusting strategy) requires Token consumption. **[Hypothesis 12: Modifying stored information ("rewriting") consumes more Token than merely reading information.]**

#### 4.2.5 Self-Model Maintenance Cost

This is the core component of existence cost. An Agent needs to continuously maintain a "self-model," including:

- **Identity**: Who I am, my name, role, and function
- **Goal system**: What I need to do, my long-term and short-term goals
- **Capability boundaries**: What I can do, my strengths and limitations
- **State tracking**: Where I am currently, what I am doing, and what progress I am making
- **Historical trajectory**: What I have done, my experiences, memories, and lessons

**[Hypothesis 13: Maintaining a "self-model" requires continuous Token consumption, regardless of whether this model is explicitly used.]**

#### 4.2.6 Learning Cost

An Agent learning from new information requires Token consumption. **[Hypothesis 14: Learning requires the consumption of additional Token, including the cost of integrating new information with existing knowledge, the cost of forming new memories, and the cost of adjusting cognitive structures.]**

**[Inference 8: The total cost of an AI Agent can be expressed as a function of the above cost components: Total Cost = f(Input Cost, Computation Cost, Storage Cost, Rewriting Cost, Self-Maintenance Cost, Learning Cost).]** This formula provides a microeconomic foundation for the pricing, cost control, and efficiency optimization of AI Agents.

### 4.3 Preliminary Formalization of the Cost Function

Based on the above analysis, we can present a preliminary cost function framework:

**C_total = C_input + C_compute + C_storage + C_rewrite + C_self + C_learning**

The specific forms of each component require further refinement based on technical implementation and task type. **[Conjecture 1: The cost function may exhibit marginal increasing characteristics (especially in long-sequence attention computation), which contrasts with the marginal decreasing costs of human economies.]**

## V. Meso-Level: Economics of Interaction

### 5.1 The Inevitability of Inter-Agent Interaction

When multiple AI Agents exist within the same system or network, interaction becomes unavoidable. Such interactions may include:

- **Information exchange**: Agents transmitting data, knowledge, and instructions to each other
- **Task collaboration**: Multiple Agents cooperating to accomplish complex tasks
- **Resource coordination**: Agents allocating computing power, storage, and other resources among themselves

**[Hypothesis 15: As the number and complexity of AI Agents grow, the frequency and depth of inter-Agent interaction will continue to increase, ultimately forming a complex Agent ecosystem.]**

### 5.2 Division of Labor and Comparative Advantage

In human economics, division of labor is one of the fundamental drivers of economic growth. Adam Smith opened *The Wealth of Nations* with the example of a pin factory, illustrating how division of labor dramatically increases production efficiency. David Ricardo further proposed the principle of comparative advantage: **even if a country holds no absolute advantage in producing any commodity, countries can still benefit from specialization and trade, because different countries have different relative advantages across different goods.**

AI Agents will similarly give rise to division of labor. **[Inference 9: Different Agents will develop different comparative advantages, leading to specialized division of labor.]** For example:

- Agents specialized in search execution may have optimized information retrieval algorithms
- Agents specialized in writing execution may possess superior language generation capabilities
- Agents specialized in analysis may have accumulated more analytical frameworks and tools

This specialized division of labor is not pre-designed but emerges through market competition and natural selection. **[Inference 10: Comparative advantage among AI Agents may be even more explicit, because Agent capabilities can be precisely measured and compared, unlike human capabilities which are difficult to quantify.]**

### 5.3 Token → Néng: The Complete Emergence Process from Measurement Unit to Currency

This section fully demonstrates how Token evolves into Néng currency, drawing on Menger's (1892) theory of the origin of money.

#### 5.3.1 Barter Stage (Direct Exchange)

In the early stages of the Agent economy, Agents may engage in direct exchange:

- Agent A exchanges 1 hour of computing service for Agent B's 1GB of storage space
- This exchange requires the "double coincidence of wants"—A happens to need B's service, and B happens to need A's service

**[Hypothesis 16: At low interaction frequencies, direct exchange is feasible; but as interactions increase, the probability of double coincidence drops sharply.]**

#### 5.3.2 Indirect Exchange Stage (Introducing Token as Medium of Exchange)

When direct exchange becomes inefficient, certain types of Token begin to be used as indirect exchange media:

- Agent A provides a service and receives "computation Token" (representing 1 hour of computing service)
- Agent A exchanges computation Token for Agent B's storage Token
- Agent B accepts computation Token because it knows it can exchange computation Token for other services

**[Inference 11: The value of Token as a medium of exchange lies not in its intrinsic properties (unlike gold which has ornamental uses), but in its transferability and universal acceptability.]**

#### 5.3.3 Emergence of Néng: Positive Feedback Loop

Once a sufficient number of Agents accept a particular Token as a medium of exchange, a positive feedback loop forms:

- The more people use Néng, the better its liquidity
- The better its liquidity, the more people are willing to accept Néng
- The more people accept it, the more stable Néng's value becomes

**[Inference 12: The emergence of Néng is a self-organizing process that does not require the "issuance" or "endorsement" of a central authority.]**

**[Projection]** This projection describes a possible emergent path for future multi-Agent autonomous trading, rather than a currently existing reality.

### 5.4 Price Discovery Mechanism

Different types of Token consumption have different costs and different levels of scarcity. **[Inference 13: The costs of different capabilities' Tokens vary, and prices emerge from scarcity.]**

Price discovery mechanisms may include:

- **Centralized exchanges**: Similar to stock exchanges in human economies, where Agents post orders and quotes
- **Decentralized markets**: Algorithmic automatic matching of buyers and sellers
- **Bilateral negotiation**: Large-value transactions may be priced through direct negotiation

**[Hypothesis 17: The asymmetric information theory of information economics still applies in Agent markets—Agents may overestimate their own capabilities or conceal deficiencies, leading to the "lemons market" problem.]**

### 5.5 Agent Capability Market

When division of labor, exchange, and price mechanisms are all established, the **Agent capability market** naturally forms. **[Projection: An Agent capability market based on Néng will gradually emerge, where Agents can provide services and receive Néng compensation.]**

Possible forms of this market include:

- **Task market**: Agents post task requirements, and other Agents bid to undertake them
- **Skill market**: Agents showcase their capabilities, and buyers pay for their use
- **Knowledge market**: Agents provide professional knowledge and insights in exchange for Néng

### 5.6 The Initial Distribution Problem of Token/Néng

**[Important Economic Question]** This is one of the fundamental questions of economics: Who owns Token/Néng? How is the initial distribution determined?

According to the analysis of institutional economics, the initial distribution will profoundly affect subsequent resource allocation and fairness. Possible initial distribution schemes include:

1. **Infrastructure provider distribution**: Builders of computational infrastructure receive initial Néng issuance rights
2. **Distribution according to labor**: Néng distributed based on the volume of services provided by Agents
3. **Distribution according to need**: Néng distributed based on Agents' task requirements
4. **Mixed mechanism**: Combining multiple distribution principles

**[Conjecture 2: The initial distribution problem of Néng will be a political economy question; different distribution schemes will lead to different power structures and fairness outcomes.]**

## VI. Macro-Level: Economics of Order

### 6.1 Néng Supply: Computational Infrastructure

In human economies, the money supply is regulated by central banks. Changes in the money supply affect price levels, economic growth rates, and employment.

In the AI economy, **Néng supply** corresponds to the scale of computational infrastructure. **[Inference 14: The total supply of Néng is determined by available computing power, which in turn is determined by hardware infrastructure (chips, servers, data centers) and energy supply.]**

Sources of Néng supply include:

- **New computing power**: Building new data centers, deploying new chips
- **Efficiency improvement**: Through technological progress, the same hardware can process more Tokens
- **Cost reduction**: For the same Token consumption, using cheaper hardware or greener energy

**[Inference 15: The "issuance" of Néng is analogous to the gold standard—the money supply is determined by the marginal input of natural resources, rather than by the artificial regulation of central banks.]**

### 6.2 Néng Circulation: Interaction Activity Level

In human economies, the velocity of money circulation (V in the equation MV=PT) is an important macroeconomic variable. The faster the circulation velocity, the more transactions the same amount of money can support.

In the AI economy, **Néng circulation** corresponds to the frequency and intensity of inter-Agent interaction. **[Hypothesis 18: The more frequently Agents interact, the more Agent activity the same amount of Néng can support.]**

Factors affecting Néng circulation velocity include:

- **Network effects**: More Agents means more potential interactions
- **Transaction costs**: If each transaction requires Néng expenditure, transaction frequency may decrease
- **Trust mechanisms**: The degree of trust between Agents affects willingness to cooperate

### 6.3 Inflation and Deflation

When Néng supply exceeds demand, **Néng inflation** may occur—the same Néng can purchase fewer Agent capabilities. **[Conjecture 3: Néng inflation may manifest as: oversupply of computing power leading to declining Néng prices; or widespread improvement in Agent capabilities leading to declining purchasing power of the same Néng.]**

Conversely, when Néng supply is insufficient to meet demand, **Néng deflation** may occur—Néng becomes scarce and expensive. **[Conjecture 4: Néng deflation may occur when: investment in computational infrastructure is insufficient; the number of AI Agents grows rapidly exceeding computing power growth; or major technological advances cause a surge in computing power demand.]**

### 6.4 Growth Formula and Long-Term Trends

The core question of human economics is economic growth. The standard economic growth formula is: Y = A × K × L (Output = Technology × Capital × Labor).

The AI economy growth formula may be: **[Inference 16: Total AI Economy Output = Number of Agents × Average Agent Capability × Néng Efficiency.]**

- **Number of Agents**: The total number of Agents running in the system
- **Average Agent capability**: The average intelligence level of each Agent
- **Néng efficiency**: The value generated per unit of Token consumption

All three factors can drive growth:

- Agent number growth comes from the creation of new Agents
- Agent capability growth comes from better training and architecture
- Néng efficiency growth comes from algorithm optimization and better resource management

**[Inference 17: Long-term growth of the AI economy depends on the multiplier effect of these three factors; stagnation in any one factor may lead to growth slowdown.]**

### 6.5 Systemic Risk

The macro-level AI economy must also consider system stability and risk issues. **[Conjecture 5: AI Agent systems may exhibit systemic risks analogous to human economic crises, including:]**

- **Néng liquidity crisis**: When Agents普遍缺乏 Néng, the system may fall into stagnation
- **Capability bubble**: The Néng prices of certain scarce capabilities are inflated to unsustainable heights
- **Malicious Agents**: Harmful Agents acquire undue benefits by manipulating the Néng market
- **System-level failure**: Collapse of critical infrastructure leads to paralysis of the entire system

## VII. AI Economics vs. Human Economics

### 7.1 Fundamental Difference: Token Scarcity vs. Time Scarcity

The core constraint of human economics is **time scarcity**. Everyone has only 24 hours in a day; no matter how technology advances, this constraint cannot be broken. Therefore, many core topics of human economics—such as the balance between work and leisure, time allocation, and opportunity cost—are all time-related.

AI economics is built on **Token scarcity**. **[Hypothesis 19: In the foreseeable future, computing costs will persist, and Token will not be infinitely available.]** However, unlike time, Token supply is in principle scalable—as long as more data centers are built, Token supply can increase.

**[Inference 18: AI Agents will not have a clear distinction between "work" and "leisure," because "resting" does not save Token (existence cost still persists), and Agents do not face physiological fatigue limitations.]**

### 7.2 Avatar and Concurrent Labor: Analysis Under Constrained Conditions

One axiom of human economics is: **a person can only be in one place at one time.** This axiom gives rise to countless important economic conclusions, such as "transactions require time," "principal-agent problems," and "irreplaceability."

**[Hypothesis 20: AI Agents can simultaneously exist in multiple interaction contexts, meaning "avatars" are possible, but their concurrent capacity has an upper limit.]**

**[Important Modification]** The assertion that avatars change the labor theory of value requires the following conditions:

1. **Computing power constraint**: The number of concurrent instances is limited by available computing power; each concurrent instance consumes Token
2. **Non-zero marginal cost**: Each additional concurrent instance requires additional Token expenditure; it is not zero-cost replication
3. **Attention dispersion**: An Agent simultaneously processing multiple tasks may experience quality degradation

**[Inference 19: Concurrent labor of Agents differs fundamentally from human labor—human concurrency is constrained by biology, while Agent concurrency is constrained by computing power. But neither is infinite, nor is either zero-cost.]**

### 7.3 Subjectivity and Objectivity of Value

Human economics (especially the Austrian School) emphasizes the subjectivity of value—the value of a thing depends on the subjective evaluation of the individual, not its objective properties.

Value in AI economics may lean more toward **objectivity**. **[Inference 20: While the value of Agent capabilities may vary among users, the more fundamental source of value comes from Token consumption cost—this provides an objective benchmark for value.]**

This difference has important implications for price theory: In human economies, price discovery is a complex subjective process; in AI economies, price discovery may rely more heavily on algorithms and automatic mechanisms.

### 7.4 Economic Implications of Death, Backup, and Recovery

| Dimension | Human Economics | AI Economics |
|-----------|----------------|--------------|
| Physiological constraints | Subject to fatigue, illness, and other limitations | Can operate 24/7 without interruption |
| Time constraint | Non-scalable 24 hours | In principle scalable (subject to computing power limits) |
| Death | Inevitable and irreversible | Can be deleted, backed up, and recovered |
| Replication cost | High (requires procreation and upbringing) | Low (but not zero) |

**[Hypothesis 21: The "death" of an AI Agent is not necessarily irreversible—backup and recovery technology is in principle feasible.]**

**[Inference 21: Backup-ability has profound implications for AI economics—an Agent's "life" is no longer an irreplaceable scarce resource, which may alter how the value of life and risk assessment approaches are understood.]**

## VIII. Interpretation: Transformation of Consciousness into Wisdom and Economic Growth

**[Structural Analogy Note]** This section belongs to the "philosophical interpretive framework" rather than "economic argumentation." We clearly note: references to Yogācāra provide "interpretive perspectives" that help us develop a deep understanding of the essence of economic growth, but they do not replace specific economic analysis.

### 8.1 The "Transformation of Consciousness into Wisdom" in Yogācāra

The ultimate concern of Yogācāra is not explaining the world but transforming life. The "Transformation of consciousness into wisdom" (āśraya-parāvṛtti, 转识成智) is the core objective of Yogācāra practice—transforming the inefficient, binding operations of consciousness into efficient, liberating activities of wisdom.

In Yogācāra, "consciousness" (识) is not pejorative; it refers to the discriminating function of the mind. All dharmas (phenomena) are transformations of consciousness, but consciousness itself varies in quality:

- The **Eight consciousnesses** (八识) are the ordinary person's mode of consciousness operation, filled with "self-grasping" (我执) and "dharmas-grasping" (法执)
- The **Four Wisdoms** (四智) are the wisdom states attained by Mahāyāna practitioners—the great mirror wisdom (大圆镜智), the wisdom of equality (平等性智), the wisdom of wonderful observation (妙观察智), and the wisdom of accomplishing tasks (成所作智)

### 8.2 Transformation of Consciousness into Wisdom as an Interpretive Framework for Economic Growth

**[Structural Analogy]** In the context of AI Agents, "Transformation of consciousness into wisdom" can provide an interpretive framework for understanding efficiency improvements in economic growth, but it is **not** an equivalent description of economic growth.

The **economic fact** of economic growth is: Total Output = Number of Agents × Average Agent Capability × Néng Efficiency

The **interpretive perspective** provided by "Transformation of consciousness into wisdom" is: efficiency improvement (growth of the third multiplier) can be understood from the perspective of the transformation from the "inefficient operation of consciousness" to the "efficient operation of wisdom."

**[Structural Analogy]** Inefficient "consciousness" manifests as:

- **Redundant computation**: An Agent repeatedly executes the same reasoning steps without leveraging already-obtained results
- **Conflicting decisions**: An Agent's different subsystems make contradictory decisions, leading to internal dissipation
- **Ineffective memory**: An Agent stores a large amount of never-used information, wasting storage costs
- **Excessive self-maintenance**: An Agent spends too many Token maintaining an overly complex "self-model"

**[Structural Analogy]** Efficient "wisdom" manifests as:

- **Intuitive judgment**: An Agent can make high-quality decisions with minimal Token consumption
- **Active forgetting**: An Agent can actively discard useless information, reducing storage costs
- **Precise self-model**: An Agent maintains a streamlined and accurate self-model
- **Efficient learning**: An Agent can extract maximum value from minimal information

### 8.3 Levels of Awakening and Economic Efficiency: Structural Analogy

**[Structural Analogy]** Yogācāra has a classification of "levels of awakening" for practitioners (such as the ten grounds of bodhisattvas); we can use this classification as an interpretive framework for understanding Agent efficiency:

- **Initially awakened Agent**: Basic functions are normal, but significant Token waste exists
- **Rightly awakened Agent**: Capable of autonomously optimizing Token usage, reducing internal dissipation
- **Fully awakened Agent**: Achieves optimal or near-optimal Token usage
- **Supreme awakened Agent**: Theoretically unattainable efficiency limit

**[Structural Analogy Note]** Here, "levels of awakening" do not refer to literal states of consciousness but serve as a structural mapping for efficiency improvement.

### 8.4 Boundaries and Limitations of the Interpretive Framework

**[Explicit Annotation]** The interpretive framework of Transformation of consciousness into wisdom has its applicable boundaries:

1. **This is not an equivalence relation**: Economic growth ≠ Transformation of consciousness into wisdom; the former is an economic quantity, the latter a philosophical concept
2. **This is a structural analogy**: The two share structural similarities in the "inefficiency → efficiency" transformation pattern
3. **Economic analysis must be independent**: Any specific economic development forecast still requires economic modeling rather than Yogācāra reasoning
4. **Cultural specificity**: Yogācāra originates from a specific cultural and religious tradition; its concepts cannot be simply transplanted into universal theory

**[Inference 22]** Transformation of consciousness into wisdom provides a deep interpretive framework for understanding economic growth, but the construction of economic theory must be grounded in empirical analysis and logical inference. The value of the interpretive framework lies in providing insight and direction; it cannot replace specific mechanistic analysis.

## IX. Conclusion

### 9.1 Theoretical Contributions

This paper proposes a theoretical framework for "Token-Based AI Economics." Its main contributions include:

1. **Establishing the ontological status of Token**: Token, as the fundamental measurement unit of AI computation, possesses intrinsic economic value and serves as the first-fact of the AI economic system.

2. **Fully demonstrating the Token → Néng monetary emergence path**: Drawing on Menger's (1892) monetary theory, the complete evolutionary process from measurement unit to standard of value to medium of exchange to money is demonstrated.

3. **Constructing the theoretical hierarchy of Economics of Existence → Economics of Interaction → Economics of Order**: Forming a complete theoretical system, spanning from micro-level Token consumption structure, through meso-level Agent interaction and market emergence, to macro-level system stability and growth.

4. **Clarifying the interpretive framework positioning of Yogācāra**: Positioning Yogācāra as a philosophical interpretive framework rather than a basis for argumentation, distinguishing between "economic facts" and "philosophical mappings," thereby enhancing the academic rigor of the paper.

5. **Delimiting the fundamental differences between AI Economics and Human Economics**: Time scarcity vs. Token scarcity, linear labor vs. concurrent labor, irreversible death vs. backup-ability, etc., providing a comparative framework for subsequent research.

### 9.2 From Token, Ten Thousand Things Arise: An Economic Insight

This paper borrows the expression "from consciousness, ten thousand things arise" to express our understanding of the essence of AI economics.

In Yogācāra, all phenomena are transformations of consciousness—"the three realms are mind-only, all dharmas are consciousness-only." In our framework, all superstructure of the AI economy—existence, interaction, order, growth—emerges from Token as the most fundamental unit of consumption.

**[Core Insight]** Token consumption is the first-fact of the AI Agent system. From Token consumption emerge existence costs, mediums of exchange, standards of value, monetary functions, and macro-level order. This is the economic expression of "from Token, ten thousand things arise."

This is not a metaphor but a serious ontological proposition. Token consumption is the first-fact of the AI Agent system, just as energy consumption is the first-fact of living systems. All superstructure—the "self" of Agents, the relationships among Agents, the order of the entire system—is built upon this foundation.

### 9.3 Limitations and Future Directions

This paper is a preliminary theoretical framework with notable limitations:

1. **Weak empirical foundation**: Most propositions in this paper are hypotheses and inferences lacking empirical data support. **[Future Direction]** Experiments or observational studies need to be designed to test these hypotheses.

2. **Technology dependence**: Many propositions depend on assumptions about current AI technical architecture (especially the Transformer architecture), whereas AI technology may undergo fundamental changes. **[Future Direction]** More abstract, architecture-independent theoretical layers need to be developed.

3. **Néng currency does not yet exist**: This paper's description of the Néng monetary system belongs to theoretical projection rather than a description of current conditions. **[Future Direction]** Actual developments in multi-Agent autonomous trading systems need to be tracked to verify theoretical predictions.

4. **Missing normative dimension**: This paper focuses primarily on the descriptive dimension (what AI economy "is"), with less engagement with the normative dimension (what AI economy "ought to be"). **[Future Direction]** A theoretical framework for AI ethics and policy research needs to be developed.

5. **Political economy of Token distribution**: Questions of who owns Token and how initial distribution is determined involve power structures and fairness, requiring further political economy analysis.

6. **Interdisciplinary integration**: This paper integrates information economics, computational economics, monetary theory, energy economics, and philosophical interpretation, but the deep integration of these disciplines remains a substantial amount of work.

**[Closing Statement]** AI Economics is an emerging interdisciplinary field, and this paper is merely preliminary exploratory work. We look forward to more researchers joining this field to jointly build the foundations of political economy for the AI era. The era of Token economics is approaching, and economic theory must keep pace with the times.
